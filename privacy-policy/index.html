§1

Who this covers

SL GPA is a GPA calculator for Sri Lankan university students — you pick your university and degree, enter module grades, and get a live GPA estimate. This policy covers the Android app (package com.scalar9.slgpa) and its web build. It's written by the person who builds the app, for the people who use it — plainly, and limited to what the app actually does.

SL GPA is an independent app. It is not run by, endorsed by, or connected to any university, and nothing it shows or exports is an official result. §14 explains the labels the app uses, including “self-reported” and “verified”.

§2

What we collect

What we hold depends entirely on how you use the app:

If you never sign in Nothing about you reaches us, apart from crash reports (§17), which aren't linked to you. Guest results live only in your device's local app storage.
If you sign in Your name and email address, from Google. We never see your password; sign-in is handled entirely by Google.
Once you save a result The degree, major, modules and grades you enter, and the GPA calculated from them.
If you verify your university email The university email address Microsoft returns when you link your university account, and the time it was verified. After that, the degree you choose as yours at that university. See §4.
If you complete your profile Your full name, name with initials, registration number and gender (male or female). Optionally: a short display name made from your own name, your batch (the academic year or intake you enrolled in), and the day and month of your birthday. We never ask for your year of birth.
If you submit a transcript The academic details read from it — modules, grades, and the name and registration number printed on it — a fingerprint of the file, the outcome of the check, and when you made each attempt. The document itself is not stored. See §5.
If you report something For a wrong or missing course, or a problem with an item on the Opportunities board: what you reported, the reason, any note you type, and your verification level at the time.
If the app breaks A crash report: the type of error, where in the app's code it happened, and details of the phone and the app, such as the model, Android version and language. It is not linked to your account, and you can turn it off in Settings (§17).
Kept on your device only Your theme choice, whether you've seen the introduction, which Opportunities items this device has already shown you, your notification settings, whether crash reports are on, and a short record of the notifications the app has worked out (§16). None of it is sent to us, and the app keeps it out of your phone's backup (§3).

We do not collect your location, contacts, NIC number, year of birth, or advertising ID, and the app contains no advertising SDK and no third-party analytics tracker.

§3

Guest mode vs. a signed-in account

You can use the entire GPA calculator, the planner and the result export without ever signing in. As a guest, every result you save is written only to your device's local storage — it never touches our servers. Uninstall the app, or clear its storage, and it's gone from the device.

Your phone's backup. SL GPA tells Android to leave it out of your phone's backup to your Google account and out of a transfer to a new phone. What it keeps on the device — a guest's saved results, settings such as the theme, and, if you are signed in, your sign-in session — stays on that device. After a reinstall or on a new phone you sign in again, and a guest's results start empty.

If you sign in, your results are stored in our database instead, scoped to your account by row-level security — no other account, including other signed-in users, can read or write your rows. If you save results as a guest and later sign in, those local results are uploaded once, at that point, and nowhere else.

Even as a guest, the app downloads public catalog data (universities, degrees, modules, grading scales) from our servers. Like any internet service, our hosting provider sees the network address those requests come from.

§4

University email verification

Verification is optional, and separate from signing in. If you choose to verify, you link the Microsoft account your university issues, through a standard OAuth flow in your browser. We read the email address that Microsoft returns, check that its domain belongs to a supported Sri Lankan university, and store the address and a verification timestamp against your profile.

What this address is, and isn't, used for
  • Used to confirm which university you attend, which is shown as a status on your own profile and opens the Opportunities board (§6).
  • Never given to recruiters, advertisers, or anyone posting on the board.
  • Never used to send you email from us.

Once you complete your profile, your full name, name with initials, registration number, gender and university are locked, so a completed profile can't later be rewritten into someone else's. If one of them is wrong, email us and we'll correct it. A display name and a batch are not locked: you can change or clear either on your profile at any time. A display name can only use words from your own name.

You can verify one university per account. Once you have, you choose your degree at that university. The app uses it to decide which results you can edit and check, and which items you see on the Opportunities board (§6). You can change it at any time until some of your results have been checked. After that, you can switch only to a degree that includes every module already checked, and your checked results move with you. For any other change of degree or university, contact us from Settings → Report a problem and we'll correct it.

§5

Transcript checks

Submitting a transcript is optional. If you do, the photo or PDF is sent to our server, which passes it to Google's Gemini API to be read into a list of modules and grades. Our own database logic — not the AI model — then compares that list against your degree's published modules and decides the outcome.

  • The file is not stored by us. We keep a fingerprint of it (so the same document can be recognised if it is submitted again), the details read from it, and the outcome.
  • If the check passes, the modules and grades read from the transcript become your checked record, shown to you under “My record”.
  • We count your attempts per day to limit abuse.

Result sheets for one subject. Instead of a transcript you can submit a result sheet your university published for a single subject — a list of students' registration or index numbers with their grades. To find your row, your registration number and, if you added one to your profile, your exam index number are sent to Google with the file. Only the row carrying one of your numbers is read into our database; no other student's number or grade on the sheet is kept by us. A result sheet prints no cumulative GPA and usually no names, so this check is narrower than a transcript check: it compares your number, the subject and the grade, and nothing else. We also keep a count of how many other accounts have submitted the identical file.

The exam index number is optional, is entered by you, and is not checked with your university. Once your profile is complete it cannot be changed after it is saved.

Read this before you upload
  • A result sheet shows other students. We keep only your own row, but the whole sheet — every student's number and grade on it — is sent to Google to be read, on the terms described below.
  • The whole page is sent to Google. We keep only the modules, grades and student details listed above, but everything printed on the document is sent to Google to be read. A transcript lists your modules, grades and student number; if yours shows anything else you would rather not send, cover or crop it before uploading.
  • How Google handles the document. We use the Gemini API's paid tier. Under Google's terms for that tier, Google does not use what is sent to improve its products. It keeps it for a limited time only to detect misuse of the service and to meet legal requirements, and then deletes it.
  • Your university. By submitting, you agree that a forged or altered document may be reported to your university. Where we have an arrangement with your university to confirm results, the upload screen says so, and by submitting you also authorise us to confirm those results with it. Where we have no such arrangement, the screen does not ask, and we do not contact your university to confirm results.
  • A confirmed mismatch can lead to your account being permanently closed.
§6

Opportunities, offers and adverts

The Opportunities board lists internships, scholarships, graduate schemes, events and student offers. It opens once your university email is verified; internships also need one semester of your transcript checked. Some items are commercial — paid placements, or links we may earn a commission from — and each one says so in words on its card. At most one item in four is commercial.

How items are chosen for you
  • The app uses your university, degree, year of study and gender to choose which items you see. This includes sponsored offers and adverts.
  • The matching happens on your device. The app downloads the board and filters it locally. We do not tell an advertiser or poster who you are, what you saw, or that you matched.
  • Gender is required to complete a profile for this reason. It is limited to male or female, is locked with the rest of your profile once you complete it, and is not shown to other users.
  • There is no advertising SDK in the app and no advertising profile of you is built or shared.

Tapping an item opens its link in your browser. From there you are on someone else's website, under their privacy policy; an affiliate link may tell that site you came from SL GPA. An item's picture is loaded from the address its poster supplied, which involves an ordinary web request to that host.

The list of items you've already seen (used for the “New” badge) stays on your device. Reporting an item is the one action that tells us which item you looked at — the report is stored with your account so we can act on it and stop duplicates.

§7

Exporting and sharing your results

You can export a saved result as an image or a PDF. The file is built on your device and handed to your phone's own share sheet; it goes only where you choose to send it, and we do not receive a copy.

  • An export shows your name (if you're signed in and have one on your profile), your university, degree, GPA and — if you include them — your modules and grades.
  • If you have set a display name, an exported image shows it in place of your full name. A PDF does not use the display name.
  • A photo you add to an export is used on your device only. It is never uploaded to us and is discarded when you close the export sheet.
  • Every export is marked as self-reported and “Not issued by the university” (see §14). Please don't remove those marks or present an export as an official document.
§8

How we use it

  • Calculate and display your GPA and classification, using the grading scale your faculty publishes.
  • Keep your saved results in sync across the devices you're signed in on.
  • Show your verification status, and open the parts of the app that depend on it.
  • Put your display name, if you set one, on the result images you export (§7) and in your birthday greeting (§16).
  • Show the notifications you allow on your phone: new opportunities in the evening, a weekly reminder to verify your results, and a birthday greeting (§16).
  • Check a transcript you submit against your degree's published module list, and keep an audit trail of that check.
  • Choose the opportunities, internships and offers you see, including sponsored offers and adverts (§6).
  • Fix the course catalog and remove bad board items when you report them.
  • Find and fix what breaks in the app, from crash reports (§17).
  • Detect and prevent abuse, such as forged transcripts or one registration number claimed by two accounts.

Your batch is not used for anything yet. It is optional, and we ask for it ahead of three planned uses: choosing the offers you see (sponsored ones included), comparing results within a batch, and matching results that a university may one day send us directly. None of these is in the app today, and no university sends us anything. If one is added, this page changes first (§13).

We do not sell your data, and we do not use your grades to target adverts.

§9

Sharing

We don't sell your personal data, and we don't give it to third parties for their own marketing. These outside services touch your data, strictly to make the app work:

  • Supabase hosts our database, authentication and server functions. Your account data and saved results are stored on their infrastructure, under our access controls.
  • Google (sign-in) and Microsoft (university email verification) process those steps under their own privacy policies.
  • Google's Gemini API reads a transcript you submit, on its paid tier — see §5 for how long Google keeps it.
  • Resend delivers an email to us when a report is filed. That email names the course or item reported; it does not contain your identity or the note you typed. The one exception is a message you send us from Settings → Report a problem: that email carries the message you wrote and your sign-in email address, so that we can reply to you. A copy of the message is also kept with your account.
  • Sentry receives the crash reports the Android app sends when something breaks, and stores them for us in the European Union. A report carries no account, and the app is built to leave out anything you entered (§17).

We may also disclose information where the law requires it, and — only as described in §5 — to your university in connection with a transcript you submitted.

§10

Retention & deletion

Guest data lives only on your device, for as long as the app is installed and its storage isn't cleared — that's within your control at all times, no request to us needed.

A signed-in account's data — profile, verification status, saved results, checked record and reports you filed — is kept until you delete your account.

To delete your account, open Settings → Delete account in the app. It is deleted as soon as you confirm: there is no waiting period, and it cannot be undone. If your copy of the app doesn't show that option, or you no longer have the app installed, email us from the address you sign in with and we will delete it for you. Email requests are handled by hand, as soon as possible; we can't promise a timeframe, because it depends on how many requests there are, but every request is handled and we email that address when it is done.

Deleting your account removes, from our servers: your saved results; your profile, verified university email and verification status; your checked record; the reports you filed and the messages you sent from “Report a problem”; the log of your transcript-check attempts; the record of any transcript check that was rejected; and the sign-in itself. Signing in again with the same Google account starts a new, empty account.

What deleting your account does not remove

If you have submitted a transcript, one record of each submission that was not rejected is kept after your account is deleted: the academic details read from the document — including the name and registration number printed on it — a fingerprint of the file, and the outcome of the check. The link to your account is severed, but this record still identifies you. Deleting your account is not the same as deleting all of your data.

It is kept as the audit trail behind a checked-record status, so that a check we made can still be accounted for afterwards. If you want this record removed as well, ask us and we will remove it — before you delete, from Settings → Report a problem (choose “Delete my account or data”), or by email afterwards, quoting your registration number.

Three more things outlast the account. A backup of the database is made every day and kept for up to 90 days, so a backup made before you deleted still contains your account until it expires. An email already delivered to us — a “Report a problem” message, with your sign-in address — stays in our inbox. SL GPA stays listed among the apps connected to your Google account, and to your university Microsoft account if you linked one, until you remove it there.

Anything already sent to an outside service — for example a transcript read by Google (§5) — is held under that service's own retention rules, which we can't shorten.

Crash reports (§17) are not linked to your account, so deleting it does not reach them. Sentry deletes each one after 30 days.

§11

Security

Every table that holds your data enforces row-level access control, so a signed-in session can only ever read or write its own rows. The fields that make up your verification status are additionally locked to server-side checks — they can't be set directly by the app, only by database logic that verifies the underlying condition itself. Connections between the app and our servers are encrypted.

§12

Age

SL GPA is built for enrolled university students. It isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone below the minimum age to hold a university-issued email address.

§14

What the app's labels mean

The app is careful about a small set of words. This is what each one does and does not claim.

Self-reported
Typed in by the student. Every grade in the calculator, and therefore every GPA on the home screen and on an exported image or PDF, is self-reported — including “what if” grades. Nobody has checked it unless it also says “verified”.
Student-entered
The same thing as self-reported, in the sentence printed at the foot of every export.
Not issued by the university
Printed on every export, verified or not. An export is made by this app from what the student entered. It is not a transcript, certificate or result sheet, and no university produced, approved or signed it.
Estimate
A GPA or classification calculated from the grades entered and the grading scale we hold for that faculty. Your university's own calculation is the one that counts; ours can differ, for example where repeat or minimum-grade rules apply.
Cumulative GPA / Semester GPA
The GPA across every graded module, and the GPA of one semester's modules. Both are estimates from self-reported grades.
Classification
The class (for example First Class or Second Class (Upper)) that the estimated GPA falls into, using the university's own thresholds where we hold them and a general Sri Lankan scale where we don't. It is an indication, not an award.
Unverified
A signed-in account that has not linked a university email.
Email verified
The account has linked a Microsoft account whose email address belongs to a university we support. It shows the person controls a university mailbox. It says nothing about their grades.
Profile complete
Email verified, and the student has entered and locked their name and registration number. Those details are the student's own statement; we have not checked them with the university. This is why the app never shows “Verified” on its own: the word always says what was checked — the email, or results.
“N subjects verified”
Shown on the home screen in place of “Profile complete” once at least one module is in the student's checked record but no whole semester is yet. It counts the modules that passed our automated check. It is not “Record checked”, and it opens nothing further on the Opportunities board.
Record checked
Every compulsory module of at least one semester of the student's degree has passed our automated check — from a transcript, or from result sheets submitted one subject at a time. For a transcript, the modules read from it match that degree's published module list; for a result sheet, the row carrying the student's own number does. It is our check of a document the student supplied — not confirmation from the university.
“Semester N verified” / “Results verified”
Every compulsory module that degree lists for that semester is in the student's checked record, each from a transcript or result sheet that passed the check. A single module checked on its own is marked “Verified” on its own row and does not make its semester verified. On the home screen, “Results verified” means every graded module in the calculator sits in such a semester.
Self-reported · Verified (on an export)
Stricter than the above: every module and grade shown matches, letter for letter, the student's checked record. The grades are still the student's own entries, and the export is still not issued by the university.
Matched to a checked transcript
The sentence used on a verified export, meaning the same as the line above.
Sponsored
On an Opportunities item: the organisation paid to appear on the board.
Partner offer
On an Opportunities item: a commercial offer where we may earn a commission if you use the link. Both labels are stated in words on the card, before you tap.
Guest
Using the app without signing in. Nothing is sent to us, and the app's data stays on the device (§3).
§15

Legal basis and your rights

SL GPA is run by an individual developer in Sri Lanka, who is the controller of the personal data described here. This section says what we rely on under Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022.

  • To give you the service you asked for: your sign-in, your saved results, your checked record, and answering a message you send us.
  • With your consent: verifying a university email, the details you enter on your profile — including gender, which is used to choose the internships and offers you see, sponsored ones included (§6), and an optional display name and batch (§7, §8) — your birthday, submitting a transcript (§5), and the notifications you allow (§16). Each of these is optional, and the app says what it is for at the point you give it.
  • For our legitimate interest in preventing abuse and accounting for our checks: locking a completed profile, refusing a registration number already in use, counting transcript-check attempts, keeping the record of a transcript check that was not rejected (§10), and acting on reports.
  • For our legitimate interest in keeping the app working: crash reports (§17). They are on from the first time the app opens, and you can turn them off in Settings at any time.

Where your data goes. None of it is stored in Sri Lanka. Our database is hosted by Supabase in India; Google, Microsoft, Resend and GitHub (which holds our backups) process data in other countries, including the United States (§9). Crash reports are stored by Sentry in the European Union (§17).

Automated checks. A transcript check is decided by our software, without a person. If you think an outcome is wrong, ask us and a person will review it.

Your rights. You can ask us for a copy of the data we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it (§10), or to review an automated outcome, and you can withdraw consent by deleting your account. A display name or a batch can be cleared on your profile at any time, without deleting anything else. Use Settings → Report a problem, or email the address below from the address you sign in with. We handle requests by hand, as soon as possible, and reply by email. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Data Protection Authority of Sri Lanka.

§16

Notifications

The Android app can show you three kinds of notification. Nothing is shown until you allow the app's notifications on your phone, and each kind can be turned off in Settings → Notifications or in your phone's own notification settings.

Every notification is worked out and scheduled on your phone. The app uses no push-messaging service, sends us no device identifier or notification token, and does not tell us when a notification is shown or tapped.

  • New opportunities, in the evening. Off until you turn it on. At most once a day, in the evening (Android chooses the exact time, between 6 pm and 11 pm), the app looks at the same Opportunities board you would see, matched to you on your phone as described in §6, and tells you if a section holds something you haven't seen yet. Sponsored and partner offers count only if you also turn on “Include sponsored & partner offers”, and a notification that includes one says so.
  • A weekly reminder to verify your results. Once your profile is complete, it is on unless you turn it off. If a semester you have fully graded in the calculator is not yet verified in your checked record, the app may remind you in the evening, at most once a week, until that semester is verified, you close the app's “Results out for Semester N?” prompt for it, or you turn the reminder off. It is worked out on your phone from your saved grades and your checked record. It may say that verified results may unlock special offers; that is not a promise of any offer, and it never shows a grade or a GPA.
  • A birthday greeting. If your profile has your birthday, the app schedules a greeting on your phone for about 8 am on the day, on unless you turn it off. It shows your display name, if you set one — never your full name — and so it can appear on your lock screen.

To check the board or your results in the evening, the app sends the same requests it sends when you open it, signed in as you; our servers see them as ordinary use of your account. When you sign out or delete your account, the app cancels these notifications and removes any that are still showing.

§17

Crash reports

When something in the Android app breaks, or the app stops responding, it can send a crash report to Sentry, a service that collects app errors for developers, so that we can find the bug and fix it. Reports are on unless you turn them off, with Settings → Privacy → Send crash reports. In the app, that switch says: “Sends the error type, code, stack trace and phone details, such as the model, Android version and language, to Sentry when something breaks. Not linked to your account.”

What a report contains. All of it, not only the examples the switch gives:

  • The error: its type, the type of whatever caused it, a code from our server where there is one, and the stack trace — the places in the app's own code that led to it, with the file names of that code — and when it happened. The error's own message is removed, except for a short list of error types whose message the app's code writes itself, never from anything you typed or anything stored in your account.
  • The phone: its manufacturer, brand and model; whether it is a phone or a tablet, or an emulator; its processor type and number of cores; how much memory it has, how much is free, and whether it is low on memory; its screen orientation; whether it is online; the Android version and build number, and whether the phone is rooted; light or dark mode; and its language, time zone, calendar and whether it uses the 24-hour clock.
  • The app: its name, version and build, whether it was in the foreground, how much memory it was using, and the version of Dart that runs it.
  • The moments before: whether the app had just gone to the background or come back, whether the phone had just gone online or offline, and whether it had run low on memory.
  • When the app itself crashes or stops responding, rather than catching an error and carrying on, Android adds more: the battery level, whether the phone is charging, and the battery's temperature; the screen's size and density; total and free storage; when the phone was last restarted; its chipset, processor speed and the processor types it supports; whether it is on mobile data or Wi-Fi, and what kind (such as 4G); the version of Android's kernel and runtime; when the app started, which permissions it holds, and whether it was installed from an app store; and, if it stopped responding, the place in the app's code where each part of it was at that moment.
  • A random number made each time the app starts, which only groups the reports from that one run of the app.

What a report never contains: your name, email address, registration number, grades, or anything else you entered; your account; what you tapped or which screens you opened. Sentry is set not to store the network address a report arrives from, or the rough location that address points to.

Not linked to you. A report carries no account, so we cannot look up the reports from your phone, and deleting your account has none to delete. When the app itself crashes or stops responding, Sentry's software on the phone also attaches a number that identifies this installation of the app; Sentry is set to delete that number when the report arrives, before the report is stored, so no report that is kept carries it. If you write to us about a crash, tell us roughly when it happened and your phone model, and we will look for it that way. Sentry stores reports in the European Union and deletes each one after 30 days.

On from the start. Reports are on from the first time the app opens, before the introduction, so that a crash there is not lost. We rely on our legitimate interest in keeping the app working (§15). Once the switch is off, the app makes no new report, and a report still waiting to be sent (one made while the phone was offline) is deleted. Turning the switch back on works at once, without restarting the app. The web build sends no crash reports.

Questions, or a deletion request?

One inbox, read by the person who builds the app. Signed in? Settings → Report a problem reaches the same inbox. To delete your account yourself, use Settings → Delete account.
